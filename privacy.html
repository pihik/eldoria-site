Eldoria - Privacy policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

  • Eldoria has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Your visits, ratings, review texts and review photos are stored on your phone only. The developer never receives them.
  • To show the map, articles, photos and the list of medieval events, the app connects to Google (map), Wikimedia (Wikipedia, Wikidata, Wikimedia Commons) and GitHub (the events list). Like any website you visit, these services see your IP address.

1. Who is responsible

Eldoria is published by Adrián Pažický. Contact: mythicforgeinteractive@gmail.com.

2. What is stored on your phone

  • The places you marked as visited and the date of each visit.
  • The places on your want-to-visit list and the date you added each one.
  • Your ratings and review texts.
  • Photos you add to a review (up to three per review), resized and kept in the app's private storage.
  • Settings: language, whether single places are shown on the map, your Near me choices (include visited places, alerts, alert distance), and the date of your last backup export.
  • The countries you chose on the Events tab, and the last events list the app downloaded, kept so that it can be shown without a connection.

None of this is sent to the developer or to anyone else by the app. You can delete a review in the app. Uninstalling the app or clearing its storage removes everything.

If you tap Share on a visited place, the app hands a short text (the place, its country, the date of your visit, your star rating and a Wikipedia link) to the sharing app you choose. Your review text and photos are not included.

3. Connections to other services

Google Maps. The map is drawn with the Google Maps SDK for Android, which sends requests to Google while you use the map. According to Google, the SDK collects, among other data, device information (such as operating system version and device model), crash information, your IP address and an SDK identifier, and may collect map interaction events such as panning and zooming. Eldoria does not ask for your location. See the Google Privacy Policy.

Wikipedia, Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons. When you open a place, its photo or its story, the app requests the data for that place from servers of the Wikimedia Foundation. Wikimedia receives your IP address, basic device and app information contained in the request, and which place you are opening. See the Wikimedia Foundation Privacy Policy.

Medieval events. The Events tab downloads one public file with the events list from the developer's website, which is hosted by GitHub Pages. The request does not contain the countries you chose; they are applied on your phone, and they are not sent to the developer. GitHub receives your IP address and basic device and app information contained in the request. See the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

Links you open. Links to Wikipedia, to a maps app, to an event organiser's page and to licence texts open in your browser or another app, which then handle your data under their own policies.

4. Contact form

The Contact screen prepares an email in your own email app. You see and send it yourself. The email contains what you write plus the app version, your phone's system and language. The developer uses it only to answer you and keeps it as long as needed for that. You can ask for it to be deleted.

5. Location and photos

Location (Near me). If you open Near me and allow it, the app asks your phone for your approximate location. It is used on the phone only, while the app is open, to work out which places are closest. Eldoria does not store it and does not send it to anyone. If you turn on the alerts, the app checks your approximate location in the same way while it is open, to show a short message when a place is close; nothing runs when the app is closed. The position is supplied by your phone's location services (on Android, Google Play services), which handle it under their own policy. The map then also shows a blue dot for your position. You can withdraw the permission at any time in your phone settings. The app does not ask for your precise location or for location access in the background.

Photos. When you add photos to a review, the app uses the system photo picker. Only the photos you choose are copied, resized, into the app's private storage. They are not uploaded anywhere. The app does not use your camera or microphone.

6. Backups

Backup file you export. The file contains your visits, your want-to-visit list, your reviews and your review photos. You decide where to save or send it; the developer does not receive it. Keep it somewhere safe, because anyone who has the file can read your reviews.

Android system backup. If automatic backup is turned on in your phone settings, Android may store the app's data in your Google account. That is controlled by your phone settings and Google, not by Eldoria.

7. Voluntary support

Some versions of the app show bank details for voluntary support of the developer. A transfer is handled by your bank; the developer sees what your bank passes on, such as your name and account number. Nothing in the app depends on it.

8. Data sources

Place data comes from Wikidata (CC0). Article texts come from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0) and are shortened in the app. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons under the licence shown with each photo. The events list is compiled by the developer from the organisers' own announcements and links to the organiser's page. Map symbols are from the Fantasy RPG GUI pack by Artsystack; the heading font is MedievalSharp by wmk69 (SIL Open Font License).

9. Children

Eldoria is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal data from anyone.

10. Your rights

The developer holds no personal data about you except what you send by email. If you are in the EU or EEA you may ask for access, correction or deletion of that data by writing to the address above, and you may complain to your national data protection authority.

11. Changes

If the app changes in a way that affects this policy, this page is updated and the date above changes.
